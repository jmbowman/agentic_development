# Success stories

Profiles of software projects that ship quickly without losing quality, especially with small teams or one developer, and the practices that seem to make it work. The aim is to find the threads common to several of them, so that the practices that matter most are adopted first, here and in other projects that use this repo's tooling.

Each profile covers:
- **Who:** the people and organization behind the project, how many people do most of the work, and how it's paid for.
- **Pace and quality:** evidence of both, such as release cadence, commit and pull-request volume, and issue load, each with the date it was observed.
- **Practices:** what the project does that seems to account for its pace and quality, each with the file, document or page it was read in.
- **Fit here:** which practices would suit this repo or its template consumers, which wouldn't, and why, with links to the tasks that take them up.

A claim is tagged by what it rests on: read in the project's own repository or docs, or reported by a third party (funding figures, for example, often are). Numbers carry the date they were observed, since they go stale quickly.
